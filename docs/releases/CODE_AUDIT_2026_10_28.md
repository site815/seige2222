# Code audit — 2026-10-28

## Status and sequence

The requested code-first audit, fixes and local final verification are complete. Source inspection, configuration checks and release-tool tests preceded Unreal/game execution. Subsequent diagnostics exposed additional fixes and obsolete fixtures, preserved below. The final current-source run passed all 218 native cases, the 117-stage Shipping playthrough and archive integrity checks. The evidence recorder reports `locally_verified`; hosted publication and remote CI status are separate from this local verification.

This review integrated the existing local work rather than replacing it. It is a broad engineering audit of the current implementation, not a guarantee that every defect has been found.

- Colony AI: development ordering, material and upgrade funding, placement ranking, transport growth/replacement and real worker logistics.
- Simulation: placement and walls, worker and vehicle occupancy, energy accounting, startup deployment, economy boundaries, combat targeting/projectiles and passive orders.

- Delivery: configuration validators, retained native regression fixtures, editor/package scripts, packaged verification, release-evidence validation and GitHub CI safeguards.

## Findings and changes

The implemented fixes below are included in the final passing native and rendered checks. The DX11 fallback limitation remains unresolved.

| Area | Finding | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI development | Upgrade requirements were not consistently included in material funding, allowing development to stall despite a valid next upgrade. | Fund the actual upgrade deficit through the existing paid production/trade rules. |
| AI logistics | Starting colonies lacked a complete autonomous path for growing and replacing their transport fleet. | Add configurable transport targets and paid factory requests, accounting for already queued vehicles. |
| AI material flow | Continuously running processors could consume intermediates required by the next construction or factory bill, while instantaneous staffing changes could repeatedly replace that plan. | Preserve concrete development bills, recognize permanently assigned crews and throttle competing optional production. |
| AI manufacturing prerequisites | More generator output cannot satisfy a transaction larger than the connected battery capacity. | Include battery storage before the specialist vehicle-factory stage; verify that paid assembly can actually start rather than treating generation alone as sufficient. |
| AI energy priority | A material-funded 60 kWh transport batch could still starve when optional processors continuously consumed available generation before the battery reached its transaction threshold. | Suspend competing optional production while the assembly transaction awaits energy. Check connected battery capacity and available generation first, and retain worker-support and defense-recovery priority. |
| AI transport funding | General construction availability correctly excluded factory reserves, but the vehicle's own funding plan then demanded duplicate ingredients. Routine resource buffers could also delay its missing import. | Count the job's already delivered ingredients exactly once, preserving pickup claims and other demands; prioritize paid import of its remaining unavailable ingredients over unrelated routine buffers. |
| Vehicle fabrication payment | A real delivery left 79.99999999999886 kg against an 80 kg bill. The existing 1e-9 payment tolerance accepted it, but raw subtraction wrote approximately -1.13e-12 kg, causing the strict save loader to reject an otherwise completed paid assembly. | Clamp an accepted debit's floating-point residual to zero, preserving the full authored fabrication escrow and strict nonnegative save validation. The new `Seige.Transport.FabricationPaymentRoundingPersists` regression covers rejection outside tolerance, exact energy and escrow payment, strict reload before/after assembly and no unfunded extra vehicle. Focused regression passed. |
| AI extraction placement | In a failed resource-combination seed, the deposit center placed the mine entrance on an impassable cliff while usable nearby plots served the same deposit. | Search a bounded set of offsets inside the authored extraction radius, require the same deposit ID, and retain normal geometry, access, reservation and payment checks. |
| AI defense recovery | Repeated civilian losses could leave an economic development plan rebuilding vulnerable facilities before reaching its planned defense milestone. Inner-ring towers could also duplicate covered approaches while outer buildings remained exposed. | Rank bounded legal tower positions by additional exposed approaches covered, respecting range and blocked shots. The authored policy now prioritizes the first paid tower when either threat type is enabled; peaceful starts retain economic ordering. Continued civilian losses escalate paid towers within the authored final target. Retain economic prerequisite recovery when neither direct funding nor trade is available. |
| Transport recovery | A cargo vehicle could keep retrying an unreachable return depot after its original delivery failed, preventing recovery even when another legitimate destination was reachable. | Select a physically reachable storage endpoint, retain the same cargo and vehicle, and persist recovery state without counting returned goods as successful delivery. |
| Local terrain routing | A valid graph route through distant geography could conceal a much shorter traversable local contour. | Compare a bounded local contour when the graph leaves the local envelope; preserve exact endpoints and the same physical slope, water and clearance checks. Retain the existing valid route if the local search fails. |
| Physical workstation return | Resuming a suspended charging task could restore an operating/building activity while the worker remained at the charger, or reuse a construction slot another worker had taken. | Require physical travel back to the actual workstation/cargo endpoint and recheck site crew capacity before resuming. Loading and unloading must happen at their physical endpoints. |
| Charging departure | A straight-line energy allowance could underestimate a real terrain detour; recalculating full routes for every fixed-step body would be unnecessarily costly. | Cache sampled route travel plus queue/docking allowance, invalidate on relevant world/crew/charger changes, and refresh near the departure threshold. The cache is derived and rebuilt on load; it grants no energy or berth. Out-of-range jobs still need a nearer charger. |
| Charging persistence | Rebuilding the derived route cache after loading shifted recharge decisions by up to 1.75 seconds because the decision phase depended on cache age. | Anchor the authored decision cadence to the saved simulation clock. Actual departures still require exact routes; cached estimates remain derived. The focused detour test now checks restored and uninterrupted simulation state at every step, and the scenario-threat test compares the complete post-load JSON state. Both passed. |
| Production refill reservations | Gross local inventory could include alloy already promised to an outbound construction pickup and suppress replenishment needed to manufacture another worker. | Use spendable local inventory for production-buffer refill demand while retaining the existing refill threshold and all physical reservation/delivery accounting. |
| Accepted export supply | In the later full-run candidate, an accepted 323.925 kg biomass export held only 300 kg at the port for roughly ten simulation hours with no inbound delivery. Production logistics consumed each new source batch before the existing shipment could be completed. | Move finite accepted trade delivery ahead of optional production in `Rules/policies.json`, retaining the higher fuel, maintenance, repair, defense and construction priorities. Focused dispatch verification and physical preserved-state recovery passed. |
| Construction transport pickup | Construction purpose reached the unloading handler selection but not pickup selection, preventing an eligible material-starved builder from travelling to load the same construction cargo. | Preserve construction intent at both endpoints. The builder still travels and loads physically, and the minimum core operating crew, route eligibility and owned-body/cargo ledgers remain intact. Focused recovery passed. |
| Local terrain routes | A distant geography waypoint could produce a valid but several-kilometer detour for a nearby worker trip, exhausting its battery while hiding a short local contour. | Compare a bounded local terrain search when the visibility-graph route leaves the local envelope. Keep the existing route when no shorter valid alternative exists; retain all physical edge checks. |
| AI worksite access | An accessible entrance could still lead to impassable operating or builder positions beside it. | Share the real worker station geometry with AI placement and require routes to and from those positions before reserving the plot. |
| Saved boolean types | JSON coercion could accept non-boolean threat toggles or a malformed cargo-recovery flag, changing scenario or ledger meaning during loading. | Require actual boolean JSON values for saved background/periodic threat toggles and the optional cargo-recovery flag. Preserve the legacy inference for saves without that recovery field and reject inconsistent recovery cargo. |
| AI placement | A pairwise placement comparator could produce a non-transitive ordering. | Use a consistent ranking so plot selection does not depend on invalid sort behavior. |
| Placement | Walls could be committed through wet terrain or occupied by idle transports/Rex. | Check water and those physical occupants before accepting the wall plan. |
| Energy | Fuel generators could consume fuel already reserved for a physical pickup. | Burn only spendable, unreserved fuel. |

| Construction cover | A queued foundation or open frame could block fire as if its finished opaque shell already existed. | Begin opaque projectile cover at the authored enclosure phase, retaining the intact landing shuttle and upgraded hull. ## Playthrough requirements

The updated `UiSmoke` route retains 117 stages. Its player-colony section starts with the finite landing kit and observes actual delivery and construction work. It orders a service hub, sensor, defense tower, solar generation, trading port and extractor. Extra defense and automatic worker reserves consume more components than the five-building economic bootstrap, so the staffed port first sells real spare starter alloy and buys 200 kg of components. Both paid shipments must physically settle before the extractor order. The route then exports extracted goods and imports a missing local resource. It records views of the completed tower from opposite angles, checks independent zoom/tilt, enters Rex's third-person mode and uses Shift to run.

The route does not inject completed player buildings, free credits or free materials. Its separate established-neighbor presentation section tests scenario loading and inspection; it is not evidence that a Starting AI can grow. That claim requires the real-terrain multi-colony and long-growth cases included in the full native suite. The bounded `NextUpdateSmoke` fixture also must not be substituted for the fresh-colony playthrough.

Import reconciliation includes actual components embodied in workers completed during shipment. Unfinished production inputs already belong to `TotalStock` at both endpoints and are not added a second time. Trading-port readiness permits the actual partial onsite crew and positive operating conditions; paid shipment dispatch and settlement remain mandatory. These are harness corrections, not grants or relaxed gameplay rules.
