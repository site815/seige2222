# seige2222 — Leaderless Areas and Scavenging

[Design index](README.md) · [Status definitions](README.md#design-status)

What remains after a player leaves a colony, and how surviving territory becomes an opportunity and risk for others.

## Leaderless territory

**Confirmed direction:**

- When the player leaves by orbital shuttle, surviving robots, city structures, outposts, and resources can remain behind.
- The remaining territory becomes an AI-controlled area run by its robots in a chaotic, leaderless state.
- This is an area-level state, not merely an abandoned city. It can include remote outposts and stockpiles across the sector.
- The intended state resembles the aftermath of a robot revolt: the robots are in control after player leadership is lost.

This describes leaving the colony by shuttle. Ordinary player logout still follows the established rule that the player's colony persists and continues operating; it does not itself establish abandonment.

**Status distinction:** Revolt was introduced as a possible colony-loss mechanic. Its triggers, prevention, and escape behavior remain open. Defining a shared leaderless aftermath does not settle those rules.

## Scavenging and raid outcomes

**Confirmed direction:**

- Stockpiled resources left in the area can be scavenged or taken through attacks.
- A sufficiently capable force can gradually recover resources from the area; the robots' presence means an abandoned area is not necessarily an undefended pile of goods.
- A heavily stocked area may provide a substantial resource gain.
- Another expedition may expend more in attacking and taking losses than the recovered goods are worth. A positive net return is not guaranteed.

The existing physical logistics rules apply: acquired goods must be transported home, carrying capacity matters, and victory alone does not transfer the stockpile. Existing automatic loot selection still applies; this idea does not add player loot-priority lists.

## Boundaries and unresolved behavior

- What the leaderless robots continue to produce, consume, repair, defend, or move.
- How chaotic behavior is expressed and whether it changes over time.
- How long structures and resources remain, and whether deterioration or eventual clearing occurs.
- Whether the former player can return or reclaim control, and what other players can do beyond scavenging.
- What happens to the sector's ownership and availability for future settlement.
- How ordinary population adjustment to job demand behaves without the core or player leadership.
- What information about defenses and stockpiles is available through sensors before an expedition.

No continuing production, guaranteed resource renewal, decay timer, reclamation right, or fresh settlement rule is established yet. The state does not reintroduce formal war between colonies.
